Uncertainty-ranked abstention beats random deferral on 13 of 14 public OpenML datasets with a defensible entity/time key, across four open, token-free configurations in three architecture families — XGBoost, LightGBM, and the tabular foundation models TabICLv2 and TabDPT — surviving Holm–Bonferroni for the gradient-boosted models and with bootstrap CIs excluding zero for the foundation models; a preregistered, held-out confirmatory replication on TableShift (9 credential-free tasks, 6 evaluable; frozen endpoint rule, K=20 split-repeats) confirms without exception that this free uncertainty score ties-or-beats a tuned learned selector (H2 CONFIRM). Beating random deferral is a low bar; the substantive findings are that the advantage holds under grouped and temporal shift, that no tabular foundation model auto-repairs it, and that the free score matches a tuned one at no extra cost — though both sit far below an oracle ceiling (median repair ratio 0.339 free vs. 0.338 tuned vs. 0.745 oracle). The repair does not restore coverage: deployment shifts are usually grouped, and moving from random to leave-group-out or rolling-origin splits leaves split-conformal coverage below its 0.90 nominal target (median worst-group coverage ≈0.81 exploratory, 0.797 confirmatory), with no tested distribution-free remedy restoring it at the majority level on either roster (H1 REJECT). We recommend plain split-conformal plus uncertainty abstention for grouped tabular shift, scoped strictly to selective reliability rather than a coverage guarantee; our own preregistered gate for certifying a directional coverage collapse was underpowered and knife-edge, evaluated to KILL, and we accordingly report that degradation as a direction rather than a robust count.
