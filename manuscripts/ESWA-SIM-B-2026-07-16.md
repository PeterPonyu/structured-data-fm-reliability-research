## 1. Handling-Editor Desk Screen

**VERDICT: DESK REJECT (return without review).**

ESWA runs a fast (~5-day) desk on ~4,300 submissions/yr with a high desk-reject rate, and its published mandate is *expert and intelligent systems applications*: a paper must present an applied system, or a technique demonstrated within a concrete application, with comparisons. This manuscript fails the screen on the journal's own load-bearing criterion, for four reasons a desk editor reaches in minutes:

1. **No expert/intelligent system is built or deployed.** The object is a *diagnostic audit* of reliability-evaluation practice (random vs. grouped/temporal splits, conformal coverage validity, selective-risk repair). Fraud/healthcare/demand are named as motivation in the intro but no application is actually carried out; the datasets are generic OpenML benchmarks selected for split-key defensibility, not a domain deployment.

2. **Methods/reproducibility DNA, not applications DNA.** The paper is organized around preregistered KILL gates, Stage-1/Stage-2 reconciliation, split-realization fragility, Holm–Bonferroni/BH-FDR multiplicity, RNG-stream root-causing. This is the vocabulary of a reproducibility/benchmarks or statistical-ML venue, not an applications journal.

3. **No applied novelty.** The recommended mechanism — uncertainty-ranked abstention on top of split conformal — is off-the-shelf selective classification; the authors explicitly frame the contribution as *packaging + auditing + honest scoping*, not a new method. ESWA weights methodological/applied novelty heavily.

4. **Self-declared negative headline.** The abstract and intro state up front that the main preregistered Stage-2 gate **KILLed** and the collapse count is fragile (majority-robust in only ~75–83% of split re-draws; 5–8/14 across conditions). For a fast applications desk this reads as an inconclusive audit, not a deployable result.

**Desk letter (paraphrased):**
> Thank you for submitting to Expert Systems with Applications. After editorial assessment we are returning the manuscript without external review. ESWA publishes novel expert and intelligent *systems and their applications*; the present work is a methodological reliability/reproducibility audit of evaluation protocols on standard benchmarks, with no deployed application system and an established (selective-prediction) repair mechanism. It is better matched to a data-mining/knowledge-discovery or reproducibility venue. We make no judgement on technical quality, which appears high; the decision is one of scope fit. We wish you success placing it appropriately.

## 2. Shadow Review (had it been sent — it would not be, at ESWA)

Provided for the fit calculus; these are the comments two typical ESWA reviewers *would* raise.

**Reviewer 1 — applied ML / decision-support leaning. Recommendation: Reject (out of scope).**
1. No end-to-end application. ESWA needs a concrete intelligent-system pipeline (e.g., a fraud or clinical-risk decision-support system) where reliable abstention under drift is the operational contribution, with cost/utility of deferral quantified. This is absent.
2. Method not novel: split conformal + `1−max p` abstention are standard. What does a practitioner *do* differently that they could not already?
3. Baselines thin for an applications venue: only random deferral and one untuned learned selector. No comparison to established selective-prediction / cost-sensitive abstention / OOD-detection baselines.
4. Actionable takeaway is deflationary ("use plain split conformal + abstention; nothing fancier helps"). Useful as science, weak as an applications headline.
5. Presentation extremely dense/hedged (Stage-1/Stage-2, KILL gates, post-hoc reinterpretation) — inaccessible to the ESWA practitioner readership.

**Reviewer 2 — conformal/statistics leaning. Recommendation: Reject / transfer.**
1. Rigor and honesty are commendable (preregistration, multiplicity correction, split-repeat fragility, RNG root-cause). But the confirmatory headline on the degradation axis is a *KILL*; the surviving claim is the abstention repair.
2. The repair is largely expected: uncertainty abstention beating random deferral is near-tautological. The oracle ceiling (0.745) vs. achieved (0.339) shows the free score captures under half the achievable selective-risk reduction — a modest, unsurprising win.
3. Applied value unclear: the deliverable is a protocol recommendation, not a system. Coverage side is explicitly negative (no distribution-free remedy restores group-conditional coverage).
4. Good target would be DMKD, a reproducibility track, or a conformal/uncertainty venue — not ESWA.

## 3. Fit Verdict

**ESWA fit score: 24 / 100.** (Low. Scope mismatch is structural, not fixable by editing.)

**Required reframing to become ESWA-viable (≈ a different paper):** rebuild around a *named, deployed intelligent-system application* — e.g., a temporal-drift fraud or clinical-risk decision-support pipeline — where the operational contribution is a reliable abstention/deferral policy; add cost-sensitive/utility framing of deferral, domain-specific baselines, and a single crisp actionable recommendation. The current audit-of-evaluation-practice framing, preregistration/KILL machinery, and generic-benchmark suite must go.

**Keep-DMKD vs switch-ESWA: KEEP DMKD (do NOT switch to ESWA).** The paper's core — reliability diagnostics, conformal validity under exchangeability breaks, preregistered gates, reproducibility discipline, benchmark audit — is squarely DMKD/methods territory and DMKD is a genuinely reasonable home. Routing to ESWA would burn a submission cycle on a near-certain desk reject. If DMKD ultimately declines, reroute to a reproducibility/benchmarks or conformal-uncertainty venue, never to an applications journal.
