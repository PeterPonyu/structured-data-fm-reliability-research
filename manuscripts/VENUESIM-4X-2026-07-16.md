# Venue Simulation (4×) — Structured-Data FM Reliability Paper

**Paper:** "Group- and Time-Split Reliability Collapse in Tabular Models is Model-Agnostic and Partly Repairable by Uncertainty Abstention" (Z. Fu).
**Content judged (Springer formatting ignored):** preregistered audit showing grouped/temporal-split evaluation reliability of tabular models (2 GBMs + 2 tabular FMs) is fragile (risk–coverage AURC degrades, split-conformal coverage drops below the 0.90 target, chiefly on temporal splits); uncertainty-ranked abstention repairs *selective* reliability model-agnostically (13/14 datasets, Holm-robust for GBMs, bootstrap-CI for FMs); honest disclosure that the preregistered conjunctive Stage-2 gate KILLed and the confirmatory/exploratory relabel is post-hoc; Mondrian add-on does not help; learned selector does not beat the free uncertainty score; TableShift-class (Folktables/ACS) external arm lands (repair replicates 4/4, collapse manifests as a subgroup coverage gap, exploratory); distribution-free remedies do not restore group-conditional coverage without the absent group key; strong cross-run reproducibility root-cause + fix.

Simulation date: 2026-07-16. Each venue embodied independently; no cross-contamination of verdicts.

## 1. NEUROCOMPUTING (Elsevier)

*Profile: ~1,600 articles/yr, ~3.1-month first decision; neural computation + ML methods; recurring conformal-prediction and uncertainty special issues.*

### Desk verdict: **SEND FOR REVIEW** (passes editorial screen)

> **Editor letter.** Your manuscript on split-protocol reliability and uncertainty abstention for tabular models falls within Neurocomputing's scope on learning-system reliability and calibration, and the tabular-foundation-model arm (TabICLv2, TabDPT) supplies the neural-computation connection our readership expects. I am sending it to two reviewers, but please strengthen the neural-methods framing up front — reviewers here will ask why two of your four configurations are gradient-boosted trees rather than neural models.

### Reviewer 1 (uncertainty/calibration methods)
1. The headline mechanism — uncertainty-ranked abstention beating random deferral — is standard selective classification (Geifman & El-Yaniv). What is the *methodological* novelty beyond a careful benchmark of an existing recipe under grouped splits?
2. Two of four configs are GBMs; the "neural" content rests on the two FMs. Please foreground the FM arm and report per-FM calibration diagnostics, not just the shared collapse-plus-repair narrative.
3. The self-declared Stage-2 KILL and post-hoc confirmatory/exploratory relabel is admirable but confusing for a reader: state in one place exactly which numbers are confirmatory and which are descriptive.
4. Conformal coverage is reported at α=0.10 only; add a coverage sweep (α∈{0.05,0.10,0.20}) so the under-coverage claim is not artifact of one nominal level.
5. Recommendation: **Major revision.**

### Reviewer 2 (conformal prediction)
1. The exchangeability-break argument (Section on validity) is correct but textbook; the interesting object is the *failure of distribution-free remedies* — promote that from an exploratory appendix to a first-class result with matched-coverage risk–coverage curves.
2. You cite subpopulation-shift and audited-CP estimators (Wang et al.; Zhou et al.) as untested. A referee-proof paper would run at least one, or explain precisely why they are out of scope rather than cite-and-defer.
3. The Mondrian negative and its key-dependence is the most original piece — but it is buried. Consider making "why uncertainty-taxonomy Mondrian is split-fragile" a named contribution.
4. Recommendation: **Major revision** (leaning accept if FM framing and one stronger remedy land).

### Fit score: **70 / 100**
### Required reframe: Lead with the tabular-FM arm and conformal/uncertainty methodology (not the GBMs); consolidate the confirmatory-vs-exploratory ledger into one table; run at least one stronger distribution-free / subpopulation-shift remedy rather than cite-and-defer.
### Bottom line vs DMKD: Faster decision and a genuine conformal/uncertainty readership, but you must sand down the "two of four models aren't neural" objection — a realistic second home, not a clear upgrade over DMKD's methods-native fit.

## 2. ENGINEERING APPLICATIONS OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (EAAI, Elsevier)

*Profile: ~2,000+ articles/yr; applications-first — every paper must anchor to a concrete engineering deployment or domain problem, not benchmark methodology.*

### Desk verdict: **DESK REJECT** (out of scope as written)

> **Editor letter.** EAAI publishes AI methods demonstrated on, and motivated by, a specific engineering application; your manuscript is a methodological reliability audit on generic public OpenML/ACS benchmarks with no engineering deployment, domain data, or application partner. I am returning it without review as a better fit for a methods-oriented journal, and would reconsider only a version built around a real engineering case study.

### Reviewer 1 (would-be, applications) — *hypothetical had it been sent out*
1. Motivating examples (fraud, demand forecasting, healthcare site transfer) are named in the introduction but never instantiated — there is no engineering system, no operational dataset, no deployment constraint (latency, cost of abstention, human-in-the-loop).
2. The abstention "repair" has an obvious engineering cost — deferred cases must go somewhere — yet there is no coverage/cost trade-off analysis grounded in a real workflow.
3. Results are AURC and conformal coverage on academic tables; an EAAI reader needs a decision-level outcome (dollars, downtime, misclassification cost) in an application context.
4. Recommendation: **Reject** (reframe to a domain deployment).

### Reviewer 2 (would-be, ML for engineering)
1. Model-agnosticism across GBMs and tabular FMs is interesting, but without an engineering target it reads as a benchmarking exercise.
2. The honest KILL disclosure is scientifically strong but, for an applications venue, reads as "the headline effect is fragile" — which an engineering audience will not adopt without a deployment that de-risks it.
3. Recommendation: **Reject.**

### Fit score: **38 / 100**
### Required reframe: Rebuild the paper around a single named engineering deployment (e.g., a fraud or predictive-maintenance pipeline with real grouped/temporal shift), report cost-of-abstention and decision-level outcomes, and demote the multi-benchmark audit to a validation section.
### Bottom line vs DMKD: A near-certain desk reject in current form; EAAI needs an application spine this diagnostic paper does not have — do not submit without a domain case study.

## 3. KNOWLEDGE-BASED SYSTEMS (KBS, Elsevier)

*Profile: ~1,350 articles/yr, fast desk decisions; knowledge-based / intelligent systems + broad ML methods; accommodating scope but expects an identifiable contribution/system.*

### Desk verdict: **SEND FOR REVIEW** (in scope; fast screen)

> **Editor letter.** The manuscript addresses reliability and uncertainty of intelligent tabular prediction systems under deployment shift, which is within KBS scope, and the reproducibility discipline is a plus. I am sending it out; reviewers will want you to sharpen what the *contribution* is, since the repair mechanism itself is an existing technique.

### Reviewer 1 (intelligent systems / ML)
1. What is the deliverable a practitioner takes away — a method, a protocol, or a benchmark? The paper oscillates between a diagnostic audit and a recommendation ("plain split-conformal + uncertainty abstention"). State the contribution as a concrete, reusable protocol.
2. The preregistered gate KILLed and the confirmatory labels are post-hoc. KBS readers will accept this only if you present a clean, positive take-away recommendation box; right now the abstract foregrounds the failure.
3. Novelty concern: abstention-beats-random and conformal-under-shift are both known. The genuinely new pieces (split-realization fragility of headline counts; key-dependence of the Mondrian mechanism; distribution-free remedies failing without the key) are underweighted relative to the collapse narrative.
4. Reproducibility (per-context RNG root-cause, provenance stamps, multiplicity correction) is excellent and should be a named methodological contribution, not a limitations subsection.
5. Recommendation: **Major revision.**

### Reviewer 2 (evaluation methodology)
1. 14 keyed OpenML datasets is a convenience suite; the 30-dataset arbitrary-key extension is explicitly "not a shift." Strengthen external validity by promoting the Folktables/ACS arm to confirmatory (a preregistered secondary grouping) rather than exploratory.
2. Please provide a decision flowchart / recommended-practice artifact — KBS values actionable guidance for building reliable systems.
3. Sign test on 13/14 is fine; add effect sizes and the integrated-selective-risk reductions in a headline table.
4. Recommendation: **Major revision** (accept-leaning if a positive protocol contribution is crystallized).

### Fit score: **66 / 100**
### Required reframe: Convert the diagnostic into a positive, packaged "reliable-tabular-prediction protocol" with a decision flowchart and a headline effect-size table; elevate the reproducibility discipline and the three genuinely-novel negatives to named contributions; lead with the recommendation, not the KILL.
### Bottom line vs DMKD: Fast desk and broad scope make it a viable Elsevier home, but KBS wants a positive method/protocol take-away this honestly-negative audit resists — winnable with a reframe, at the cost of the paper's candor.

## 4. PATTERN RECOGNITION (PR, Elsevier)

*Profile: ~700–900 articles/yr; selective methods journal; deep reject-option-classification (Chow's rule, Geifman/El-Yaniv) tradition and strong conformal precedent — the most topically-native of the four, with the highest novelty bar.*

### Desk verdict: **BORDERLINE — LEAN DESK REJECT** (topical fit high; novelty threshold not obviously met)

> **Editor letter.** Reject-option classification and conformal prediction are core Pattern Recognition topics, so your subject is squarely in scope. However, Pattern Recognition requires a clear methodological or theoretical advance, and the manuscript's central mechanism (uncertainty-ranked abstention) is an existing technique evaluated on tabular benchmarks; combined with the authors' own disclosure that the preregistered gate KILLed, I am inclined to return it without review. I will send it out only if you can articulate a novel methodological contribution in a rebuttal.

### Reviewer 1 (reject-option classification)
1. The reject-option / selective-prediction framing is exactly PR's tradition, but the paper contributes no new rejection rule, no new risk–coverage estimator, and no new theory — it benchmarks the free uncertainty score against a learned selector and finds a tie. For PR that is a negative/null methodological result.
2. The strongest PR-native contribution would be the *split-conditional* reject-option analysis (how the optimal rejection region moves under leave-group-out shift). This is latent in the data but not developed formally.
3. The exchangeability-break and distribution-free-remedy-failure results are correct but presented diagnostically, not as a theorem or a new estimator PR readers can cite.
4. Recommendation: **Reject** (topically ideal, but no methodological novelty).

### Reviewer 2 (conformal prediction / statistical ML)
1. Strong empirical rigor (preregistration, Holm–Bonferroni, split-repeat fragility, provenance) — better than most PR submissions on process. But PR rewards a new method, and the honest confirmatory/exploratory relabel signals the primary hypothesis did not survive.
2. Tabular-only scope with no image/signal/structured-recognition content is adjacent to, not central to, PR's typical remit; the reviewer pool may be a poor match.
3. If reframed around a *new* group-robust selective-prediction or conformal estimator (validated against the same collapse), this would be a strong PR paper. As a diagnostic, it is under-threshold.
4. Recommendation: **Major revision at best, reject more likely.**

### Fit score: **60 / 100** (topical fit ~85, realistic acceptance probability pulls the composite down)
### Required reframe: Add a genuine methodological contribution — a split-conditional reject-option rule or a group-robust selective-risk estimator — and prove/validate it against the documented collapse; recast the audit as motivation, not as the paper. Without a new method, PR is a desk-reject risk despite perfect topical alignment.
### Bottom line vs DMKD: The most topic-native venue on paper (reject-option + conformal heritage) but the worst novelty-bar match — PR wants a new method this deliberately-diagnostic paper does not offer, so submit only after adding one.
