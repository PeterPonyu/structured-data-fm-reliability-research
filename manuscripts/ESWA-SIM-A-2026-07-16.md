## 1. Handling Editor — Desk Screen

**VERDICT: DESK REJECT (do not send to review).**

**Decision letter (simulated):**

> Dear Dr. Fu,
>
> Thank you for submitting your manuscript to Expert Systems with Applications.
>
> After an initial assessment by the handling editor, I regret to inform you that your manuscript falls outside the scope of Expert Systems with Applications and cannot be sent for review.
>
> ESWA publishes papers presenting **novel expert and intelligent systems and their application to substantive real-world problems**, with a clear, deployable contribution demonstrated on an application domain. Your manuscript is, by its own framing, an *empirical reliability/reproducibility study* of tabular-model evaluation methodology: it audits how split-conformal coverage and selective-risk behave under grouped/temporal splits on 14 public benchmark datasets, and reports a preregistered kill-gate analysis. This is a valuable methodological contribution, but it is a **benchmarking / evaluation-methodology audit**, not the design and application of an expert or intelligent system to a specific real-world problem. The datasets (adult, electricity, house_sales, …) are used as evaluation vehicles, not as an application; no decision-support system is built, deployed, or validated against a domain baseline; and the headline results are, in the authors' own words, largely negative or descriptive (Stage-2 gate "KILLed", grouped-gap count "fragile/underpowered", Mondrian "does not help").
>
> Work of this kind is better suited to a data-mining / machine-learning methods venue (e.g. Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery, Machine Learning, or a conformal-prediction / reproducibility track). We encourage you to submit there.
>
> We wish you success in placing this work.

**Editor reasoning (brutally realistic):**
- The one-line pitch — "abstention repairs grouped-split reliability of tabular foundation models, with preregistered kill-gates" — reads to an applications editor as *methodology about evaluation*, not *an application*. No expert system, no decision-support pipeline, no domain problem owner.
- ESWA's ~5-day desk screen with a high desk-reject rate exists precisely to filter methods/benchmark-audit papers. This is a textbook filter case.
- Aggravating factors that make desk-reject near-certain, not marginal: (a) the abstract leads with negative/hedged framing (KILL, underpowered, fragile) that signals "audit," not "solution"; (b) heavy preregistration/RNG-discipline machinery that an applied editor will read as a statistics-methods paper; (c) no application section, no practitioner deployment, no cost/decision framing; (d) "Affiliation to be added" on the title page signals an unfinished submission.
- Probability sent to review at ESWA as currently written: **~10–15%**.

## 2. Reviewers (hypothetical — only if the editor had erred and sent it out)

### Reviewer 1 — Applied data-science / decision-support
1. The paper never instantiates an expert or decision-support system. What is the system? Abstention-to-defer is a plausible human-in-the-loop deferral mechanism, but it is evaluated as a statistic (integrated selective risk), not as a deployed decision workflow with a domain owner and an operating point.
2. No application domain is developed. "Healthcare risk," "fraud," "demand" appear only as motivating sentences in the intro; the experiments are on generic OpenML benchmarks with no domain cost model, no analyst workload, no deferral-budget analysis.
3. The contribution is overwhelmingly negative/descriptive (gate KILLed; gap count fragile; Mondrian fails; FMs do not auto-repair). ESWA readers want a deployable, positive recommendation with demonstrated value.
4. No comparison to any application-side baseline or incumbent decision system — only internal method-vs-method (uncertainty score vs learned selector vs random).
5. **Recommendation: Reject** (out of scope; no application, no system).

### Reviewer 2 — Applied ML
1. Methodologically the study is careful and honest — the RNG-reconciliation, split-repeat robustness, and preregistered kill-gate discipline are genuinely above the norm. But the honesty foregrounds fragility, which reads as "the effect is not robust" to an applied reader.
2. The single robust claim ("abstention beats random deferral 13/14") is a low bar and the paper itself concedes it; the practically interesting quantity — how much of the random→grouped coverage gap is closed — is explicitly *not* measured.
3. Novelty is contested by the authors' own related-work section (De Melo Costa 2026, BeyondArena 2026, Cheng 2025 all report the same "FM high-accuracy / low-reliability" pattern). The delta is the split-conditioning + abstention audit, which is incremental over concurrent work.
4. Actionability for an ESWA practitioner is thin: the takeaway "use plain split-conformal + abstention, not FM/Mondrian" is a *negative* configuration guideline, not an engineered solution.
5. **Recommendation: Reject / transfer to methods venue.** Technically sound but venue-mismatched.

- Add an **application-side value story**: deferral-budget vs risk-reduction operating curve, analyst-workload framing, cost of a missed decision. — *Mostly re-analysis of existing selective-risk arrays; borderline on "no new experiments" but doable from frozen records.*
- Change the abstract/title from audit-language ("collapse," "KILL," "fragile") to system-language ("a reliability-guarded selective-prediction system for tabular decision support under deployment drift").

Even fully reframed, desk-reject risk stays **moderate-to-high** because the empirical core remains a benchmark evaluation with no bespoke deployed system.
